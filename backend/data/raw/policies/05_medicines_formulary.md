# What is the NHIS Medicines List

The NHIS Medicines List (sometimes called the NHIS Medicines Formulary) is the official list of medicines that the National Health Insurance Authority will reimburse facilities for, when prescribed in accordance with the Standard Treatment Guidelines.

# How a member should use it

- Medicines on the list, when prescribed at an accredited facility, must be provided to the member without out-of-pocket payment.
- The exact medicines available depend on the level of care: a CHPS compound stocks fewer items than a Teaching Hospital.
- If a prescribed medicine is on the list but the facility is out of stock, the facility should either source the medicine for the member or refer them to a higher-level facility — not ask the member to buy it externally as a routine practice.
- Brand names are not guaranteed; the Authority reimburses based on generic name and strength.

# When the member may have to pay

- The medicine is not on the NHIS Medicines List (e.g. many newer cancer therapies, ARVs handled by NACP, certain biologicals).
- The medicine is on the list but is not appropriate for that level of care and the member is not willing to be referred.
- The member requests a specific brand name when a generic equivalent is available and being offered.

# How to verify a medicine

- Check the medicine name, generic name and strength against the NHIS Medicines List for the year in question.
- Confirm the level of care — a regional or teaching hospital usually has access to a wider range than a health centre.
- If unsure, ask the facility's NHIS desk to show the entry on the formulary.
