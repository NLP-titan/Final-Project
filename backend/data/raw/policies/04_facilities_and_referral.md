# Levels of care under NHIS

NHIS contracts with facilities at the following levels:
- CHPS compounds (Community-based Health Planning and Services) — primary, community level.
- Health Centres and Polyclinics — primary outpatient services and basic maternity.
- District Hospitals — broader OPD/IPD services, basic surgery and full maternity care.
- Regional Hospitals — broader specialist services and emergency care.
- Teaching Hospitals — tertiary, specialist, sub-specialist services.
- Mission, Quasi-government and accredited Private facilities at equivalent levels.

# Accreditation

- Only facilities accredited by the National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) can provide services to NHIS members on credit (i.e. without charging the member up front).
- Accreditation is renewed periodically. A facility's status can be Active, Suspended (under investigation or for non-compliance) or Not Accredited.
- The NHIA publishes the list of accredited facilities by region.

# Referral pathway

- Members are expected to access care at the lowest appropriate level first (CHPS or health centre) and be referred upward as clinical needs require.
- Self-referral to higher levels (e.g. directly to a Teaching Hospital for routine OPD) may not be reimbursed, except in emergencies.
- A formal referral letter from a lower-level provider is generally required for elective specialist outpatient care at regional and teaching hospitals.

# Out-of-area care

- Members can access services at any accredited facility nationwide while their membership is active. There is no geographic restriction on which accredited facility they can use.

# Emergencies

- In emergencies, the nearest facility (accredited or not) should provide stabilisation. Reconciliation and reimbursement procedures apply afterwards in line with NHIA emergency policy.
